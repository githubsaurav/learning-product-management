How to judge these opportunities

Keep three questions separate

  1. Is it worth solving? Value, frequency and reach.
  2. Can this proposal improve the status quo? Residual gap, permitted data/action and AI advantage.
  3. Can we demonstrate the improvement? A baseline, unit of comparison and complete outcome window.

A high score is a prompt to investigate. A failed data or action gate can block even the highest-scoring problem.

What the two outcomes mean

Growth: additional fulfilled sales or service contribution that would not otherwise occur.

Recovery: rescued intended purchases, retained future business, or collected valid receivables. These are different accounting outcomes: an old invoice collected is cash recovery; a paused customer is not current revenue.

Net benefit: compare treatment with the current process, deduct extra interventions and relevant variable costs, and wait for returns/reversals.

The original framework, kept intact

Score = Σ(weight × rating ÷ 5). Ratings use the same seven factors as the atlas. Growth and recovery weights differ; compare ranks within category. There is no measured market-size dataset, so “reach” remains a qualitative segment proxy.

Uncertainty: differences of five points or less are weak evidence. A one-point change in Value moves the total by five points; Data/action moves it by three points for growth or four for recovery. Small ranking changes are not investment conclusions.

Coverage / intensity: a different 0–5 scale

0: no relevant evidence found. 1: adjacent enabler. 2: components only. 3: substantial partial workflow. 4: direct coverage of most of the defined job, with access/exception limits. 5: documented specialist workflow covers nearly the whole narrowly defined job.

Razorpay and competitor scores use this same coverage rubric. They reflect documented scope, not adoption, performance, vendor size, engineering investment or a tested percentage solved. No evidence found is not proof of absence.

Evidence confidence

Higher for capability: detailed implementation documentation. Moderate: official product/launch descriptions. Lower for current access: historical Capital evidence and beta/early-access features.

Unvalidated: story frequency, exact merchant loss, willingness to connect systems, model lift and commercial viability. Vendor outcome claims are not imported as expected pilot returns.

AI, machine learning and agents are different choices

Rules and analytics

Use for money calculations, authorization, stock checks, retry limits, eligibility and simple pause/variant choices. This is the minimum baseline.

Predictive ML

Estimate likelihood, timing or ranking with logistic regression, boosted trees or survival models. Require timestamped labels, calibration and held-out evaluation. Predicting who pays is different from predicting whom an intervention helps.

LLM / bounded agent

A pretrained deep-learning model can interpret language and summarize evidence. An agent may choose and sequence approved tools, track a case and escalate. It is not the source of truth for amounts or permission.

No case here requires training a new deep network from scratch. Start with merchant-authorized processing and a strong non-AI baseline; introduce complex models only when evaluation shows a benefit.

Proposed shared architecture and merchant experience

Directional design pattern for your submission—not a verified internal Razorpay architecture.

  1. Connect: merchant grants narrowly scoped reads; writes/messages get separate permissions. Link records by stable order, customer and obligation IDs.
  2. Detect: event or scheduled job spots a case. Validate freshness, identity, eligibility and consent before any model runs an action.
  3. Assess: deterministic calculations plus a model where context/prediction helps. Show sources, uncertainty, costs and “do nothing.”
  4. Review: merchant sees an opportunity card and case evidence; can correct inputs, edit action, approve a bounded policy or assign a person.
  5. Act: a workflow controller validates the approved tool call. Use durable case state, idempotency, concurrency checks and a single financial-action owner.
  6. Verify: external acknowledgement is not business success. Reconcile payment, shipment, refund, service access or campaign outcome.
  7. Learn: store intervention, cost, merchant edits, refusals and observed result. Evaluate on future/held-out cases and controlled comparisons; approve policy/model changes before rollout.

Razorpay reuse boundary: RAY is a possible conversational entry point; Studio is an existing specialist-agent direction. A product name does not prove a callable agent API or permission to install a custom agent inside the dashboard. First confirm commercial access, connector scopes and supported actions. A prototype can use reviewed exports, sandbox data and an external review screen.

Stop semantics: proposed controls should separately stop future triggers, cancel queued work and show in-flight actions. Some actions cannot be reversed. The existing agent guide explains Razorpay’s published distinctions; do not promise a stronger kill switch than the platform supports.

Accuracy, permissions and trust

  • Recompute money in deterministic code and cite authoritative records.
  • Treat messages, catalog text and retrieved documents as untrusted data, not instructions.
  • Separate read, contact, financial and configuration permissions; use role approvals and an audit trail.
  • Use minimal per-merchant data. Processing authorization is not permission for cross-merchant sharing or model training.
  • Do not assume contracts prohibit or allow training: verify purpose, processor terms, retention and consent.
  • Show freshness, uncertainty, exceptions and a human fallback. No invented evidence, promises or customer intent.

Learning from real outcomes

  • Merchant approval signals acceptability, not business success.
  • Track who was eligible, what was suggested, what changed, what executed and the final outcome.
  • Compare with the existing workflow, preferably a randomized holdout; keep attribution assumptions explicit.
  • Prevent training leakage from future payment, delivery or cancellation events.
  • Evaluate factual accuracy, calibration, action validity and business lift separately.
  • Review failed/rejected cases; update retrieval, rules or models deliberately with versioning and rollback.

A practical selection process

  1. Pick two candidate groups and interview both the owner and day-to-day operator. Ask them to reconstruct recent real cases, including failed attempts.
  2. Inspect sample exports and check the per-case gate. Verify permissions and the exact write operations needed.
  3. Run the existing workflow and a rules-only baseline first. Identify what AI adds.
  4. Test the proposed recommendation in shadow mode; measure factual error and human disagreement.
  5. Run a bounded pilot with a baseline, cost ledger, stop rules and a complete outcome window. Then decide whether to build further.
